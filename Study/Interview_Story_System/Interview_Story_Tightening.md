**Bottom line up front:** the feedback was not about your experience, which mapped well (SaaS, client-facing delivery, multi-config environments, good closing questions). It was one fixable flaw: your answers ran long and got murky, especially on East West and Aptean, and the interviewer had to ask clarifying follow-ups. In an implementation role, crisp, structured communication is the job, so rambling reads as a yellow flag. Here is the fix, using your own facts.

## The rule (say it, then stop)
Meat first, then stretch. Say the point in the first two sentences, then stop and let them ask for more. Do not narrate the whole timeline. Your spine: (1) the claim, the result in one sentence; (2) one proof; (3) the relevance to this role.

---

## Story 1: East West Manufacturing (your customer-side implementation)
**Claim (lead with this):** "I led the ERP implementation and data migration that moved East West Manufacturing, a five-site global manufacturer, onto Aptean Intuitive."

**One proof:** "I ran the onboarding, compliance, financial, and training sessions, supported the Mexico offices in person in El Paso and Juarez, and owned the ETL and data migration onto the new system."

**Relevance:** "That is this role: driving a structured implementation and keeping the customer moving through it, on schedule."

- **30 seconds:** claim + one proof + relevance. Then stop.
- **60 to 90 seconds:** add the multi-site and international scope and the training cadence, one sentence each.
- **The layoff, in one neutral line if asked:** "My role wrapped when the migration finished and the team consolidated." No defensiveness, no over-explaining.

## Story 2: Aptean (your vendor-side breadth)
**Claim:** "At Aptean, the software vendor itself, I implemented and supported more than 80 clients across the US, the Midwest, and Europe."

**One proof:** "I managed a wide range of configurations, from legacy on-premise setups to the cloud version of the same product, so I adapt fast to whatever a customer's environment is."

**Relevance:** "That range is exactly why I ramp quickly on any customer, no matter their setup."

---

## Your differentiator (the standout line you were missing)
The feedback said you were credible but not memorable. This is your sharp line, and it is true:

> "I have implemented the same kind of software from both sides of the table: as the vendor onboarding 80-plus clients, and as the customer leading a five-site, cross-border migration. So I see the whole board, and I know where implementations actually break."

Use it once, early, as your hook. It is a differentiator most candidates cannot claim.

## Practice this week
- Record the two stories at 30 seconds each. Tally any hedges ("I guess, kind of, just") and cut them.
- Say the claim, one proof, one relevance, then stop. If they want more, they will ask (the compress rule).
- Log it in the rehearsal log in your workbook. Three clean reps and it becomes automatic.
