# Daily Companion
### One page to open every day. Tailored to how you actually work: top-down, purpose-driven, non-linear.

**How to use this:** do the daily routine below, most days. On a low-energy day, just do the three anchors, one Anki set, and one minute of reading aloud. That still counts. Momentum beats intensity, and safety beats speed.

---

## The three anchors (say them daily)
- **Regulation:** "My brain is trying to keep me safe, but I am secure enough to handle this." Three slow belly breaths, feet on the floor.
- **Purpose:** "I help people adopt and understand technology safely." When motivation dips, it is a meaning problem, not a discipline problem.
- **Consultative:** "I am not selling myself. I am diagnosing their problem."

## The daily routine (about 20 minutes)
1. **Regulate (2 min).** Three belly breaths. Read your Purpose anchor and your one near goal out loud.
2. **Study rep (5 min).** Today's Anki cards (one deck), plus one short foundations drill (subnetting or a small coding problem) if you are in that phase.
3. **Speaking rep (5 min).** Read a few sentences aloud, slowly and over-enunciated, and record one minute. Or run this week's drill: meat-first, hedge count, or the 30-60-120 story ladder. Standing, on camera.
4. **Focus block (start small).** Set a 25-minute timer, pick the single smallest next task, and start before you feel ready.
5. **Recover.** After anything socially draining, shrink the next session, do not skip it.

## Interview rehearsal scripts (from your "Bumbling to Boardroom" plan)
Full plan: the Public Speaking Transformation Plan; banks in `interview_prep/` (Answer and Question Bank, Project Delivery Stories, 10-Minute Pre-Interview Checklist, Repair Kit). The daily essentials:

- **Story bank status: 22 stories, all four modes.** `Project Delivery Interview Stories.md` covers implementation, both-sides breadth, delivery, process, recovery, innovation, cross-cultural, prioritization, conflict, failure, feedback, analytics, executive communication, scope surprises, quality judgment, executive persuasion, churn, adoption, release coordination, opposing stakeholders, and rapid ramp. Every story has full, CART, short, and PREP.
- **Before any interview, read the lane lead-in guide first** (bottom of the story bank, also on side two of `Interview_Story_Card.docx`). It names the opener, the exact first line, and the backup order for implementation, CSM, program management, and pre-sales. Selection under pressure is what cost you the Procare screen, not content.
- **Rotation drill:** pick two stories a day, record each at 30 seconds in PREP mode, cut the hedges, log the pass. Eleven days covers the whole bank.

- **The one rule: meat first, then stretch.** Say the point in your first two sentences, every time, at every length. Add length by stacking prepared modules behind the point, never by delaying it. Thirty seconds = point plus one proof; four minutes = the same point plus more evidence.
- **The meat-first spine:** (1) the claim, the result in one sentence, start with the ending; (2) one proof, a concrete detail that earns it; (3) the relevance, why it matters for this role. Then stop, or stretch.
- **The time ladder (one answer, any length):** 30s = anchor only; 60 to 90s = anchor + career arc + why this role; 2 min = add your strongest story in three sentences; 4 min = add a second story + a forward-looking close. Compress rule: if they look ready to move on, finish the current module and end on your why-this-role line.
- **Your five tells to kill (targets, not flaws):** buried outcome, stream of consciousness, hedging qualifiers ("I guess / kind of / just"), warm-up wandering, and volunteering salary. Record yourself and tally them.
- **20-second umbrella pitch:** "I take on ambiguous, cross-functional work, program, product, process, or system, and turn it into structured delivery that actually gets adopted. My best work is at the intersection of business, operations, and technology, where things usually break, and I have owned that from both the vendor and the customer side." Rehearse to automatic.
- **Interview them back:** ask about management style, autonomy, and how decisions and changes are explained. You thrive with why-driven managers.
- **On the morning of:** run the 10-Minute Pre-Interview Checklist. Rehearse only the 30-second anchor, breathe, read your Evidence Log once, and expect the pre-performance spike (it means it is working).

## Tips tailored to you (your operating principles)
- **Lead with the why and the point.** Your top-down mind is a strength; answer first, then support.
- **Attach a purpose to everything.** You perform far better when a real goal is attached; supply the meaning yourself.
- **Prepare and script.** Running on a rehearsed script is a strength, not cheating.
- **Manage the senses.** Reduce glare and noise, keep water nearby, recover after exertion.
- **Be kind to the stumble.** A fumble is a rep, not a verdict.
- **Delegate and tool the low-level.** Outsource fine-motor, linear, and admin work; let AI write the steps you skip.

## Weekly review (15 minutes, once a week)
Progress against the long-term goal, one adjustment, and celebrate one finished thing. Completion is what rebuilds your follow-through.

## Weekly job-search hour (referral-first, not spray)
Fewer, warmer, better-targeted beats mass applications. Full detail in `Job_Search_Playbook.md`.
- **3 to 5 targeted applications** to Strong or Adjacent-fit roles only; for each, message one real person at the company.
- **2 to 3 thoughtful comments** on posts from your target lanes (see `People_and_Lanes_to_Follow.md`).
- **1 to 2 personal connection requests** (target-company employees, former colleagues).
- **1 reconnect** with a warm contact who could refer you (former Aptean or East West colleagues).
- **1 build-in-public post** on what you learned or built this week (SQL, Security+, a container demo).
- **Log it** in your tracker.

## Decision framework (for hard-choice days)
- **Why filter:** does this serve the long-term purpose? If not, drop, shrink, or delegate it.
- **Reversible vs irreversible:** move fast on reversible decisions; slow down only on the few you cannot undo.
- **Time-box:** give the choice a deadline and a good-enough bar, decide, act, refine.
