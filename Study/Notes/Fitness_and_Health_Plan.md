## The plan in one sentence
Nutrition drives the weight down; low-impact cardio protects your knee and lungs while it burns; strength rebuilds the muscle and the chest; and daily walks with Pepito keep the streak alive.

## Cardio (low-impact, knee and asthma friendly)
- **Repurpose the elliptical.** Take the hoodies off it. It is the single best tool you own for this: real calorie burn with almost no knee impact. Start 10 to 15 minutes at an easy, conversational pace, build toward 25 to 30.
- **Walk Pepito** 20 to 30 minutes on most days. This is your keystone habit and your zone-2 base.
- **Warm up, breathe, build gradually.** Keep your inhaler nearby, avoid cold/dry or high-pollen days when you can, and stop if you get tight or wheezy. As fitness improves, the shortness of breath eases.
- **Vibration plate:** useful for warm-up, mobility, balance, and recovery, not a primary fat-loss tool. Do not count on it for weight loss.

## Strength (3 days a week, full-body, dumbbell)
Your 35 lb dumbbells are plenty for a while if you use slow tempo and one-arm work. Master form first, then add reps, then tempo, then sets, then load (a loaded backpack works until you buy heavier bells).

**Warm-up (5 min):** easy elliptical or march in place, arm circles, hip circles, glute bridges, cat-cow.

**Day A**
- Goblet box squat (sit to a chair or step, only to a depth with zero knee pain): 2 to 3 x 8 to 12
- Dumbbell floor press (chest): 2 to 3 x 8 to 12
- One-arm dumbbell row (back): 2 to 3 x 8 to 12 per side
- Glute bridge (glutes and hamstrings, knee-friendly): 2 to 3 x 12 to 15
- Standing dumbbell shoulder press: 2 x 8 to 12
- Plank or dead bug (core): 2 to 3 x 20 to 40 seconds

**Day B**
- Dumbbell Romanian deadlift / hip hinge (light, hamstrings and glutes): 2 to 3 x 10 to 12
- Incline push-up (hands on the step) or knee push-up (chest): 2 to 3 sets, stop a rep or two short
- Chest-supported or bent-over dumbbell row / reverse fly (upper back): 2 to 3 x 10 to 12
- Low step-ups to the platform IF pain-free, otherwise glute-bridge march: 2 x 8 per side
- Dumbbell curl and overhead triceps: 2 x 10 to 12
- Side plank or bird-dog (core): 2 to 3 sets

**Knee rules:** no pain during or after. Reduce depth and range before you reduce effort. Strengthening glutes, quads, and hamstrings gently tends to help the knee over time. If it flares, ice and elevate; if it persists, see a doctor or physical therapist.

**Chest note:** the floor press and push-ups build the chest muscle; overall fat loss is what changes the appearance. Both are in this plan.

## Sample week
- **Mon:** Strength A + 10 to 15 min elliptical
- **Tue:** Walk Pepito 20 to 30 min
- **Wed:** Strength B + mobility
- **Thu:** Elliptical 20 min easy + short walk
- **Fri:** Strength A (or B)
- **Sat:** Longer walk or park time, something you enjoy
- **Sun:** Rest, gentle mobility, vibration plate

## Nutrition (framework, not a rigid diet)
- **Protein at every meal** to keep muscle while you lose fat and to control hunger: chicken, turkey, lean beef, eggs, fish (salmon, tuna, not shellfish), Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, tofu, beans. No pork and no shellfish, easily worked around.
- **Vegetables at most meals** for fullness, fiber, and blood pressure.
- **Carbs, your choice of two that both work:** lower-carb or keto (it worked for you before), or moderate natural carbs (fruit, oats, rice, potatoes, beans) kept mostly around workouts. Pick the one you will actually sustain; consistency beats the specific ratio.
- **Fats in moderation:** olive oil, avocado, nuts, eggs.
- **Hydrate well** (helps blood pressure, sweating, and hunger) and **go easy on high-salt processed foods** for your blood pressure.
- **Aim for a modest, sustainable deficit**, roughly 1 to 2 pounds a week. Slow and steady keeps muscle and is far easier to keep off than a crash. A registered dietitian can personalize this, especially with your blood pressure.

## Track the wins (this is where your brain locks in)
- Weekly: weight (same day and time), waist measurement, and blood pressure if you have a cuff.
- Each session: your reps and weights, so you can see progress.
- Daily: the walk streak with Pepito.
- **Non-scale wins matter most early:** easier breathing on stairs, less sweating, looser clothes, better sleep, steadier mood. These usually show before the scale moves much. Celebrate them.

## Adjusting on hard days
A walk with Pepito is a complete, successful day. Ten minutes on the elliptical counts. Shrink the session, do not skip it. Momentum beats intensity, and safety beats speed.

## Week-by-week progression (Weeks 1 to 8)
The rule of progress, in order: master form, then add reps, then add tempo, then add sets, then add load. This block walks you up that ladder safely. Walk Pepito most days the whole time; that never changes. Strength is 3 days a week, alternating Day A and Day B. Never trade knee pain or wheezing for a number.

| Week | Strength focus | Cardio (elliptical) | This week's one change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 sets each, reps at the LOW end. Form and pain-free range are the only goals. | 10 to 12 min easy, 2x | Just show up and learn the movements |
| 2 | 2 sets, reps to the MID range | 12 to 15 min, 2x | Add 1 to 2 reps per set vs week 1 |
| 3 | Add a 3rd set on the two main lifts (squat and a press) | 15 to 18 min, 2 to 3x | +1 set on the main lifts |
| 4 | 3 sets on all main lifts, reps to the TOP of range | 18 to 20 min | Reps to the top of every range; mini re-test (count reps) |
| 5 | 3 sets, add 3-second lowering (tempo) on squat, floor press, RDL | 20 to 22 min | Introduce tempo (tempo makes 35 lb harder with no extra weight) |
| 6 | Tempo on all main lifts; add load where a lift feels easy with clean form (35s or a loaded backpack) | 22 to 25 min; optional gentle intervals if asthma is comfortable and cleared | Add load on the lifts that are ready |
| 7 | 3 sets, tempo, new heavier loads settled in; reps back to MID at the new weight | 25 min | Solidify the new loads |
| 8 | Full 3 sets, tempo, top reps at current loads. Your best week. | 25 to 30 min | Re-test: compare reps and weights to week 1 |

**End of Week 8, re-measure:** weight, waist, blood pressure (if you have a cuff), and how your breathing feels on stairs. Then start Block 2: run the same 8-week shape beginning at your new week-8 loads and reps, and consider a heavier pair of dumbbells or an adjustable set (your 35s will start to run out).

**If life happens or the knee flares:** repeat the week, do not skip ahead. Missing days is normal; the win is coming back. Any time you feel wiped out, take an easy deload week (2 lighter sets, more walking) and resume.

**Nutrition runs in parallel the whole time.** The weight comes mostly from the eating framework above; the training is what keeps your muscle and rebuilds the chest and back while the fat comes off.
