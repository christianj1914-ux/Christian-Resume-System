## The core advice, synthesized across all of them
1. **The market is candidate-flooded.** As the recruiter put it, there are far more candidates than roles. Standing out is the whole game; being merely qualified is not enough.
2. **LinkedIn is your discovery engine.** Optimize it, tell your career story, and you should be getting recruiter messages weekly if your profile and skills match the jobs you want. Cut vague soft-skill "junk" (hardworking, problem-solver) from your skills; recruiters search by concrete keywords and certifications.
3. **Certifications, chosen strategically.** There is a real tier list of which certs matter in 2026. Recruiters literally search by cert (Security+ came up repeatedly). Get certs that map to the tools and roles you are targeting; do not collect a cert for a technology you will never use.
4. **The cybersecurity path is concrete:** foundational IT plus hands-on labs plus networking, then Security+. Government and defense roles need US citizenship and a clearance (background check). Expect continuous learning. And the human is always the weakest link, someone always gives up their password.
5. **Build projects, not just tutorials.** Start with YouTube tutorials, then take them further, add a twist or extra difficulty so your project is not the same one 30,000 other people built. Build a portfolio, especially while between jobs.
6. **Business and people skills beat pure technical.** You have to get people to like you to get things done. Likability, relationships, and communicating value are what convert skill into offers and promotions.
7. **Know your salary anchor number before you interview,** not after. Decide your number up front and negotiate from it.
8. **Networking and referrals win.** You need someone to put your name in rooms you cannot enter. Several speakers had not applied to a job in years because referrals and reputation carried them.
9. **Personal brand and content are leverage and income.** With your own media platform you make far more, and your reputation opens doors. The entrepreneur-plus-content path recurs as the way to escape pure salary.
10. **AI, used well.** Do not mass-apply with AI spam (recruiters see it and it backfires). AI strategy and AI governance are rising lanes; keep upskilling; humans stay in the loop.
11. **Continuous work, and take care of yourself.** It is a long race; sustainability and self-care are part of winning it.
12. **Business Analysis, Product Management, and Cloud Governance** are repeatedly named as accessible, high-paying lanes, often reachable from a non-traditional background.
