# Daily Confidence and Consultative Delivery Practice
### 15 minutes a day. Builds calm and skill in the same sitting.

You are not broken. Interview nerves are a trainable survival response, and the fixes are skills. This routine works two levers at once and in equal measure: the nervous-system tools that keep you steady, and the consultative delivery that makes you persuasive without feeling like you are selling. Keep it small enough that your anxiety never flags it as a threat.

## The two anchors (memorize both)

- Mindset: "My brain is trying to keep me safe right now, but I am secure enough to handle this."
- Consultative: "I am not selling myself. I am diagnosing their problem."

## Your positioning anchor (broad identity, sharp per role)

You are not a master of none. Your "one thing" is a capability, not a domain: you take ambiguous,
cross-functional problems and turn them into structured delivery that people actually adopt. That
shows up as program management, product ownership, continuous improvement, consulting, or ERP
implementation depending on the role. You specialize in the messy middle where business, operations,
and technology have to line up, which is exactly where most initiatives break. Broad in where it
applies; specialized in the capability. Full detail in "Recruiter call analysis and repositioning.md".

Your 20-second umbrella pitch (over-rehearse until it is automatic and calm):
"I take on ambiguous, cross-functional work, program, product, process, or system, and turn it into
structured delivery that actually gets adopted. My best work is at the intersection of business,
operations, and technology, where things usually break, and I have owned that from both the vendor and
the customer side."

Then match ONE title to the role in front of you (Technical Program Manager, Product Owner, Continuous
Improvement Analyst, or Implementation/Solutions Consultant) and lead with the proof story that fits
that lane (see the answer bank's "Part 0" for the per-lane proof map).

Three reframes to say at full size, never apologetically:
- Not "I'm not tied to one tool/ERP" but "I ramp fast because I understand the logic and the business
  process; the interface changes, the fundamentals do not."
- Not "I'm not really a coder" but "configuration, integration coordination, and SQL-level data work;
  I bridge business and dev."
- Not "almost desktop support" (Aderant) but "enterprise application support with SQL Server, Active
  Directory, and integration diagnostics."

Ban these self-sabotage words: "just", "almost", "not really", "I wouldn't advertise that". They take
a strength and apologize for it.

## The 15-minute routine

**Minutes 0 to 2, warm up and prime.** Read a paragraph aloud slowly, then read the top three wins from your Evidence Log out loud. This warms the voice and reminds your nervous system, before you practice anything, that you are competent.

**Minutes 2 to 7, the week's speaking drill.** Run this week's focus from your speaking plan: meat-first (answer in sentence one), the hedge count (record and tally I guess / kind of / let me know if you want more), or the 30-60-120 (one story at three lengths). One drill, out loud, standing, on camera.

**Minutes 7 to 10, a consultative rep.** Pick one. Either rewrite one of your answers problem-first, opening with the client's bottleneck (Client problem, then what I did, then the outcome), or draft three diagnostic questions you would ask a real client or the interviewer. This trains the doctor-patient stance: listen and diagnose before you prescribe.

**Minutes 10 to 13, a mindset rep.** Pick one. Write down one anxious prediction and label it a theory, not a forecast. Or run the self-talk reframe: name one thing you are hesitant to claim, then say why sharing it is a service, not bragging. Or name one thing you are avoiding and shrink the next step until it is almost too small to refuse (open the document for two minutes).

**Minutes 13 to 15, ground and score.** Say both anchors once. Then a three-item scorecard on today's practice: did I lead with the point, what was my hedge count, did I frame it as their problem first.

## Interview-morning grounding sequence

Run this before a real call, not daily. Your one-page "10-Minute Pre-Interview Checklist" is the fast version of this; run that in the ten minutes before the call, and use the "Interview Answer and Question Bank" the night before to pick your signature story and your three questions.

- Lower the bar: rehearse only your one 30-second anchor, out loud, standing. Not the whole guide.
- Separate feelings from facts: whatever your anxiety predicts, treat it as a theory to test, not a done deal.
- Name the intent: say the mindset anchor. The nervousness is protection, not truth.
- Expect the spike: your anxiety may climb right before you perform. That is the extinction burst. It means the change is working, not failing.
- Read your Evidence Log once, especially the praise section. End on proof, then breathe.

## Weekly focus (rotate, pairs with your speaking plan)

- Week A: meat-first and problem-first framing.
- Week B: kill the hedges and run the self-talk reframe.
- Week C: the story ladder and diagnostic questions.
- Week D: full mock on camera, then score against the three-item card.
