# Recruiter call analysis and repositioning plan

From the Robert Half screen with William Whatley (2026-07-21). Typos decoded: Aptian = Aptean,
Adorem = Aderant, Encompass = Encompix, Intuitive = Aptean Intuitive ERP.

## The one thing underneath everything: you undersell, and it has become a positioning problem

The recruiter confirmed the "master of none" trap is real, not just in your head, "too technical for
the generalist roles, not the exact ERP for the specialist roles." But read the rest of his notes
and a clearer truth shows up: this is a framing problem, not a fit problem. Every time you had a
strong card, you played it face-down.

- "Although officially I was just supposed to be tech support..." (Aptean) - you led with the
  disclaimer, then buried the real story.
- "Almost desktop support sometimes" (Aderant) - you talked your most technical role down.
- "I'm not really a coder... I wouldn't advertise that" - you volunteered a weakness nobody asked
  about.
- The 100-client scale, the tariff/sourcing decisions, the finance-plus-IT ownership under the CFO -
  all mentioned once, in passing, never as headlines.
- The recruiter had to push you multiple times to get the technical depth out.

This is the exact same pattern your interview debriefs flagged (buries the lead, hedges, undersells),
only now it is operating at the level of your whole identity, not just individual answers. Same
disease, higher stakes. The good news: the cure is the same energy applied one level up.

## The reframe: you are not a generalist. You are a rare dual-sided ERP implementation translator.

Here is what you actually are, and almost nobody else is: someone who has lived ERP implementations
from both sides of the table.

- Vendor side: at Aptean you were the consultant on the ERP itself, roughly 100 clients, pre-sales
  discovery, demos, RFPs, SOWs, coordinating an India dev team on deployments.
- Client/owner side: at East West you owned that same class of ERP end to end, under the CFO, running
  finance integrations (EFT/Truist), SQL and Power BI, global supply chain, inventory, vendor audits,
  and cross-border operations in Mexico and El Paso.

That combination is genuinely rare and genuinely valuable. It means you understand why
implementations succeed or fail from both the vendor's and the customer's chair, and you bridge the
two languages that never talk to each other: business/finance and technical/IT. That is not "a bit of
everything." That is a specific specialty: the person who translates between the ERP and the business
and makes the implementation actually land. Claim it.

## Pick one title and lead with it

Stop answering "what are you looking for" with a range. The recruiter told you plainly that leading
with one title builds confidence. Yours is:

Primary: ERP Implementation Consultant. Secondary/interchangeable: ERP Business Analyst.

Why these fit and the specialist/generalist trap does not: they are valued for functional knowledge,
cross-team communication, and full-lifecycle implementation experience over deep coding. They align
with your PMP-in-progress and your Aptean consulting background. And they are the natural home for
someone with dual vendor/client ERP experience. Search broader if you want, but lead with this one
title in every recruiter and interview conversation.

## Turn your three "weaknesses" into your three strongest lines

1. "Not one specific ERP" becomes transferable ERP fluency:
   "I ramp on any ERP fast because I know the underlying logic, GL, PO, inventory, BOM. The interface
   changes; the business process does not." This flips the objection ("you don't have our ERP") into
   a strength before they can raise it.
2. "Not really a coder" becomes the implementation-consultant sweet spot:
   "My technical work is functional configuration, integration coordination, and SQL-level data work,
   not software development. I am not a developer, but I work closely with dev teams and read and
   write SQL for migrations and troubleshooting." Honest, specific, and correctly positioned. Never
   say "I'm not really a coder" or "I wouldn't advertise that" again.
3. "Just desktop support" (Aderant) becomes what it actually was:
   "Enterprise application support with SQL Server, Active Directory, and third-party integration
   diagnostics." It was your most technical role; the recruiter said so. Stop shrinking it.

## The self-sabotage phrases to delete (this is the core daily drill now)

Ban these from your vocabulary. They are the strategic version of the hedges your debriefs caught:
- "just" (just tech support)
- "almost" (almost desktop support)
- "not really" (not really a coder)
- "I wouldn't advertise that"
- "much more involved than we were led on" - say the involvement as the headline, do not frame it as
  a surprise or a caveat.

Every one of these took a strength and apologized for it. The fix is not bragging; it is stating what
you did at full size and letting the interviewer decide. Sharing accurate evidence is a service to
them, not conceit.

## Deeper interview advice

- Open ERP conversations with the dual-sided story, then breadth. Never open with a list of ERPs.
  Script: "I have supported Aptean Intuitive from both sides, as the vendor consultant at Aptean and
  as the internal owner at East West. That gives me a complete picture of how implementations succeed
  or fail." (The recruiter essentially wrote this line for you.)
- Lead with technical depth proactively. The recruiter had to pull it out of you; do not make an
  interviewer dig. Put SQL, Power BI, integration coordination, and data migration up front.
- Attach a number to every role, every time. 100 Aptean clients, 150+ users across five sites, $1M+
  at-risk revenue recovered, 78% manual-work reduction, the Mexico/El Paso cross-border operations.
  A role without a number sounds smaller than it was.
- Use structured lifecycle language for implementation work: discovery, requirements gathering, data
  migration, configuration, UAT, training, go-live and hypercare. "Full implementation lifecycle" is
  the phrase; it signals a capability, not a story.
- Pre-empt the "not our ERP" objection with the transferable-logic line before they voice it.
