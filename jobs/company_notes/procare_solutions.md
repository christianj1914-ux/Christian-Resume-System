### Raw Notes
Interview with Procare Solutions, Tue 08/04/2026. Scheduled via Emily Fitzmaurice / schedule@flax.greenhouse.io.

Panel: Nancy (Manager of Operations, Professional Services) and Courtney (Supervisor, Professional Services). Role title on this call: Professional Services Consultant. This differs from the "Implementation Manager - ChildPlus" and standard "Procare Implementation Manager" reqs assumed going in; this appears to be a third, distinct req on the Professional Services org chart.

Tone: warm, fairly relaxed. Nancy did most of the talking. Opened with clear interest in Christian's background: "You have quite the extensive background." Closing verdict was positive but tempered: "You have a lot of experience. This would be a little bit different." They explicitly flagged the role as a step down in complexity from his background, not a step up.

What landed:
- Implementation methodology answer: discovery, requirements mapping, phased timelines, post-go-live training. Came across clearly.
- Honesty about the one project that stretched to 13 months: named insufficient server/infrastructure discovery upfront as the root cause. Read as self-aware, not defensive.
- AI answer was the strongest moment: used Claude for ideation and Codex for execution to automate an inventory adjustment workflow with duplicate-detection logic. Interviewers visibly engaged: "We are at ProCare getting heavily involved with AI... I'm glad that you're comfortable utilizing it."
- Handling unresponsive clients: personal touch landed (partner is an assistant principal, understands school-side chaos). Proposed incremental 30-minute deliverables instead of overwhelming clients with everything at once.
- Change-resistant clients: framed around client confidence and word-of-mouth, not just task completion.

Deeper read on the implementation / project management answers specifically:
Nancy repeatedly probed whether Christian had truly led implementations end-to-end (not just supported them). Her question progression: "Did you lead a project from the beginning to the end and do the training and the implementation of that whole project?" then "How long were your projects like?" then "Maybe some of the steps that you had to take to achieve that goal." Christian gave a credible sequence: discovery, current-state review, requirements mapping, technical dependency checks (internal infrastructure, third parties, customizations), phased timelines, training, and post-go-live support. He explicitly said current-state review matters because he wants to see "exactly what their rule for opportunities, what we're working well and what has room for improvement," and that he would have clients "walk me through the steps of their current system so I can see not only what it's capable of, but the user technical confidence."

Best line of the section: Christian argued post-implementation training can matter more than the implementation itself if user adoption fails: "The post implementation training, which is more important in my experience than actual, the implementation as well."

Nancy's response after hearing the full sequence: "So it sounds like you did a lot of building the system out first and then actual training on the system," then, crucially: "Sounds like you had a methodology that you managed and customized when needed." That line signals she accepted the methodology claim. After that point in the conversation, her questions shifted away from "did you really do this" and toward customer behavior, missed calls, flexibility, and workload, i.e. she stopped testing capability and moved to testing fit.

Honest downside on this section: the implementation examples came from manufacturing / ERP / infrastructure-heavy contexts. Procare's own product was described by the interviewers as "out of the box," "very straightforward," and not highly complex. So while the answer proved competence, it likely reinforced the fit question rather than resolving it: is this person built for a more operational, high-volume onboarding role rather than a deep custom implementation role. The rigor and thoroughness that read as strength in an enterprise ERP interview may have read as heavier than necessary here, where speed, flexibility, and keeping less-organized clients moving matters more than deep systems analysis.

Concerns and honest gaps:
- 100-client caseload within a roughly 90-day implementation window is genuinely new territory. Christian acknowledged directly, said post-implementation support gave some parallel experience but conceded "that would be something new and challenging."
- Audience mismatch is real: background is manufacturing ERP, their environment is childcare/school-district SaaS, lighter software, more client wrangling than build complexity. Interviewers noted this explicitly.
- Connection dropped mid-interview during Courtney's explanation of the software; some content likely missed.
- Christian had to step away to answer the door mid-call; minor disruption to flow, but transcript shows no negative commentary during that window, only Nancy and Courtney discussing their own schedules and time off.

Key stated message from Nancy on what they want: "I always want someone when we interview them to understand our audience. We have to be flexible."

Close: Nancy: "It's a pleasure talking with you." HR to follow up with next steps.

Overall read: they were pressure-testing fit and pace, not capability, and the implementation-methodology exchange is the clearest evidence of that. Nancy tested capability first (did he really run projects end-to-end), got satisfied ("you had a methodology"), then spent the rest of the conversation testing pace/audience fit instead. Strongest answer: the AI/workflow automation example. Second-strongest and most decision-relevant answer: the implementation methodology walkthrough, which resolved the capability question but likely opened the fit/complexity question. Weakest answer by outcome (not delivery): the 100-client/90-day caseload question, honest but confirmed the panel's core fit concern without pairing it with a concrete adaptation plan.
